The basic ideas behind our government, and the kinds of government it is compared with.

The Big Ideas

IdeaMeaning
Popular sovereigntyPower comes from the people.
RepublicPeople elect representatives.
Rule of lawEveryone must follow the law.
Separation of powersPower is divided among three branches.
Checks and balancesEach branch can limit the others.

Types of Government

TypeMeaning
Direct democracyCitizens vote on laws directly. Example: ancient Athens.
RepublicCitizens elect representatives. Example: the Roman Republic.
MonarchyA king or queen rules, often by inheritance.
Divine rightThe idea that a ruler gets power from God.
DictatorshipOne ruler holds power, and people have few freedoms.

The Constitution sets up the structure, powers, and limits of the government.

The Preamble

“We the People” means the government gets its power from the people and is accountable to the people. The Preamble lists the purposes of government.

PurposeMeaning
Establish justiceCourts and fair laws
Ensure domestic tranquilityKeep peace at home
Provide for the common defenseProtect the country
Promote the general welfareSupport the public good
Secure the blessings of libertyProtect freedom

The Three Branches

BranchMain jobs
Legislative: Congress (Article I)Makes laws, declares war, taxes, coins money. Congress is the House of Representatives and the Senate.
Executive: the President (Article II)Enforces laws, can veto bills, Commander in Chief of the military.
Judicial: the Courts (Article III)Interprets laws. The Supreme Court is the highest court.

Key Rules

TermMeaning
VetoThe president rejects a bill. Congress can override a veto with a 2/3 vote in both houses.
ImpeachmentThe House brings the charges. The Senate holds the trial and can remove the official.
Supremacy ClauseFederal law is above state law. If they conflict, federal law wins.
Habeas corpusThe government must give a reason for holding someone in jail.
Civil lawDisputes between people or groups, such as contracts, property, or slander.
Criminal lawCrimes against society.

The ideas and documents that shaped our government.

Document or ideaWhat to know
Mayflower Compact (1620)Early self-government.
John LockeAn Enlightenment thinker: natural rights and the social contract. His ideas influenced the Declaration of Independence and the Constitution.
Declaration of Independence (1776)Main author: Thomas Jefferson, inspired by Locke. People have natural rights, government must protect them, and people can change or overthrow a government that fails.
Constitution (1787)Main author: James Madison.
Great Compromise (1787)House: representation by population. Senate: equal representation.
Northwest Ordinance (1787)Guided westward expansion and banned slavery in that territory.
Monroe Doctrine (1823)Warned Europe to stay out of the Western Hemisphere.

Supreme Court decisions and major events that changed how the Constitution is applied.

Government and Federal Power

CaseWhat it decided
Marbury v. Madison (1803)Established judicial review: the Supreme Court can declare laws unconstitutional.
McCulloch v. Maryland (1819)The national bank was constitutional, and federal law is supreme over state law.
Gibbons v. Ogden (1824)The federal government controls interstate commerce.
United States v. Nixon (1974)Executive privilege is not absolute. The president must obey the law.
Bush v. Gore (2000)Stopped the Florida vote recount in the 2000 presidential election.

Civil Rights and Equal Protection

CaseWhat it decided
Dred Scott v. Sandford (1857)Enslaved people were not citizens and could not sue in federal court, and Congress could not ban slavery in the territories. It increased conflict before the Civil War.
Plessy v. Ferguson (1896)Upheld segregation under “separate but equal.”
Korematsu v. United States (1944)Upheld Japanese internment during World War II.
Brown v. Board of Education (1954)Separate schools are inherently unequal. Overturned Plessy in public education.

Rights of the Accused

CaseWhat it decided
Mapp v. Ohio (1961)Evidence from an illegal search cannot be used in court.
Gideon v. Wainwright (1963)Defendants in criminal cases have the right to a lawyer, even if they cannot afford one.
Miranda v. Arizona (1966)Police must inform suspects of their rights before questioning.

Speech, Religion, and Students

CaseWhat it decided
Schenck v. United States (1919)Government can limit speech that creates a clear and present danger.
Engel v. Vitale (1962)School-sponsored prayer in public schools is unconstitutional.
Tinker v. Des Moines (1969)Students keep free speech rights in school, as long as they do not substantially disrupt learning.
Hazelwood v. Kuhlmeier (1988)Schools can censor school-sponsored student speech for valid educational reasons.
Texas v. Johnson (1989)Flag burning is protected symbolic speech.

Privacy and Individual Liberties

CaseWhat it decided
Roe v. Wade (1973)Recognized a constitutional right to privacy in abortion decisions. Overturned by Dobbs v. Jackson Women’s Health Organization (2022).
District of Columbia v. Heller (2008)Protected an individual right to have a firearm for lawful purposes like self-defense.

Major Laws and Events

Law or eventWhat to know
Interstate Commerce Act (1887)Federal regulation of transportation and trade.
OSHA (1970)Workplace safety rules.
Watergate (1972–1974)Led to President Nixon’s resignation. Showed that even the president is not above the law.
