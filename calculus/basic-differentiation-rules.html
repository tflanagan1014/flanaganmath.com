Lesson 14: Basic Differentiation Rules

Derivative of a Linear Function

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[mx + b] = m \]

The slope of the tangent line to a line is the slope of the line.

Derivative of a Constant

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[c] = 0 \]

The slope of the tangent line to a constant function is always zero.

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[x] = 1 \]
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[-x] = -1 \]
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[7x - 3] = 7 \]
\[ \frac{d}{dx}[12] = 0 \]

Arguably the most widely used, and longest remembered, rule from calculus tells us how to differentiate a power of x.

The Power Rule

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[x^n\right] = n x^{n - 1} \]

You will not be asked to prove this rule on an exam.

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[x^5\right] = 5x^4 \]
\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[x^2\right] = 2x \]
\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[x^{100}\right] = 100x^{99} \]

Two properties of exponents let us use the power rule on roots and reciprocals.

\[ x^{-n} = \frac{1}{x^n} \]
\[ x^{\frac{m}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{x^m} \]

Differentiate \(f(x) = \sqrt{x}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} f(x) &= x^{\frac{1}{2}} \\ f'(x) &= \frac{1}{2}x^{-\frac{1}{2}} \\ &= \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} \end{aligned} \]

Differentiate \(f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} f(x) &= x^{\frac{1}{3}} \\ f'(x) &= \frac{1}{3}x^{-\frac{2}{3}} \\ &= \frac{1}{3\sqrt[3]{x^2}} \end{aligned} \]

Differentiate \(f(x) = \frac{1}{x}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} f(x) &= x^{-1} \\ f'(x) &= -x^{-2} \\ &= -\frac{1}{x^2} \end{aligned} \]

This matches what we found with the limit definition.

The Constant Multiple Rule

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[c\,f(x)] = c\,f\,'(x) \]

The Sum and Difference Rule

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[f(x) \pm g(x)] = f'(x) \pm g'(x) \]

Prove the constant multiple rule.

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dx}[c\,f(x)] &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{c\,f(x + h) - c\,f(x)}{h} \\ &= c \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h} \\ &= c\,f'(x) \end{aligned} \]

Sample Exam Problems

Differentiate \(f(x) = 4x^6 - 7x + 18\).

\[ f'(x) = 24x^5 - 7 \]

Differentiate \(f(x) = \dfrac{24}{\sqrt[4]{x^3}}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} f(x) &= 24x^{-\frac{3}{4}} \\ f'(x) &= 24 \cdot \left(-\frac{3}{4}\right)x^{-\frac{7}{4}} \\ &= -18x^{-\frac{7}{4}} \\ &= -\frac{18}{\sqrt[4]{x^7}} \end{aligned} \]
