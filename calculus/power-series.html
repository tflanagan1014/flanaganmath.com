Lesson 63: Power Series and the Interval of Convergence

A power series, like \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} c_k x^k\), is an infinite polynomial. For some values of \(x\) it converges, and for others it diverges. The values where it converges form its interval of convergence.

Idea: Use the Ratio Test to find the interval. Then check each endpoint separately, because the Ratio Test is inconclusive there.

Find the interval of convergence of \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^k}{k + 5}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{x^{n+1}}{n + 6} \cdot \frac{n + 5}{x^n}\right| &= |x|\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n + 5}{n + 6} \\ &= |x| \end{aligned} \]

The series converges when \(|x| < 1\). Now check the endpoints.

  • At \(x = 1\): \(\sum \frac{1}{k + 5}\) diverges, by limit comparison with the harmonic series.
  • At \(x = -1\): \(\sum \frac{(-1)^k}{k + 5}\) converges, by the Alternating Series Test.

The interval of convergence is \([-1, 1)\).

Find the interval of convergence of \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^k}{2^k(k + 1)}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{x^{n+1}}{2^{n+1}(n + 2)} \cdot \frac{2^n(n + 1)}{x^n}\right| &= \frac{|x|}{2}\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n + 1}{n + 2} \\ &= \frac{|x|}{2} \end{aligned} \]

The series converges when \(|x| < 2\). Now check the endpoints.

  • At \(x = 2\): \(\sum \frac{1}{k + 1}\) is the harmonic series, which diverges.
  • At \(x = -2\): \(\sum \frac{(-1)^k}{k + 1}\) converges, by the Alternating Series Test.

The interval of convergence is \([-2, 2)\).
