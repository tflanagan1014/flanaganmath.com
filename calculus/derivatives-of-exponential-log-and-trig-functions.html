Lesson 15: Derivatives of Exponential, Log, and Trig Functions

Like \(\pi\), the number \(e \approx 2.71828\) appears throughout mathematics and the sciences: in continuous interest, the normal distribution, and Euler’s formula.

\[ e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta \]

Derivative of the Exponential Function

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[e^x\right] = e^x \]

It is its own derivative! The derivative of “e to the x” is “e to the x.”

Prove this rule using the limit definition.

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dx}\left[e^x\right] &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{e^{x + h} - e^x}{h} \\ &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{e^x\left(e^h - 1\right)}{h} \\ &= e^x \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{e^h - 1}{h} \\ &= e^x \cdot 1 \\ &= e^x \end{aligned} \]

We found \(\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{e^h - 1}{h} = 1\) when we studied limits.

Differentiate \(f(x) = e^x - 5x + 2\).

\[ f'(x) = e^x - 5 \]

Derivative of an Exponential Function

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[a^x\right] = (\ln a)\,a^x \]
\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[2^x\right] = (\ln 2)\,2^x \]
\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[10^x\right] = (\ln 10)\,10^x \]

Derivative of the Natural Log

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[\ln x] = \frac{1}{x} \]

Derivative of a Log Function

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[\log_a x\right] = \frac{1}{x \ln a} \]

We will prove these rules later in the course. They are very important!

Find \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left[\log_7 x\right]\).

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[\log_7 x\right] = \frac{1}{x \ln 7} \]

Derivatives of the Trigonometric Functions

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dx}[\sin x] &= \cos x & \frac{d}{dx}[\cos x] &= -\sin x \\ \frac{d}{dx}[\tan x] &= \sec^2 x & \frac{d}{dx}[\cot x] &= -\csc^2 x \\ \frac{d}{dx}[\sec x] &= \sec x\tan x & \frac{d}{dx}[\csc x] &= -\csc x\cot x \end{aligned} \]

Differentiate \(f(x) = 5\sin x - 2\cos x\).

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= 5\cos x - 2(-\sin x) \\ &= 5\cos x + 2\sin x \end{aligned} \]
