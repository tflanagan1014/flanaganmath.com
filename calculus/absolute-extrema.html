Lesson 24: Critical Numbers and Absolute Extrema

A critical number is a number in the domain of \(f(x)\) where \(f\,'(x)\) is zero or undefined. There, the graph has a horizontal or vertical tangent line, so this is where the function might have a maximum or minimum.

Find the critical numbers of \(f(x) = 3x^3 - 5x^2 - 12x + 20\).

\[ f'(x) = 9x^2 - 10x - 12 = 0 \]

Use the quadratic formula.

\[ \begin{aligned} x &= \frac{10 \pm \sqrt{100 + 432}}{18} \\ &= \frac{10 \pm \sqrt{532}}{18} \\ &= \frac{5 \pm \sqrt{133}}{9} \end{aligned} \]

Find the critical numbers of \(f(x) = \ln x\).

\(f\,'(x) = \frac{1}{x}\) is never zero. It is undefined at \(x = 0\), but 0 is not in the domain of \(\ln x\). So there are no critical numbers. Within the domain is important!

A continuous function on a closed interval has both an absolute maximum and an absolute minimum. They occur at a critical number or an endpoint.

Finding Absolute Extrema on \([a, b]\)

  1. Find the critical numbers in the interval.
  2. Make a table of values with these critical numbers and the endpoints.
  3. The largest function value is the absolute maximum.
  4. The smallest function value is the absolute minimum.

Find the absolute extrema of \(f(x) = 4x^5 + 5x^4 - 40x^3\) on \([-1, 3]\).

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= 20x^4 + 20x^3 - 120x^2 \\ &= 20x^2\left(x^2 + x - 6\right) \\ &= 20x^2(x + 3)(x - 2) \end{aligned} \]

The critical numbers are \(0\), \(-3\), and \(2\), but \(-3\) is not in the interval.

Find the absolute extrema of \(r(\theta) = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \theta - 2\cos\theta\) on \([0, 2\pi]\).

\[ \begin{aligned} r'(\theta) &= 1 + 2\sin\theta = 0 \\ \sin\theta &= -\frac{1}{2} \\ \theta &= \frac{7\pi}{6},\ \frac{11\pi}{6} \end{aligned} \]
