Lesson 17: The Chain Rule

Often one quantity depends on another, which depends on another. The chain rule differentiates a composition of functions: the derivative of the outside, evaluated at the inside, times the derivative of the inside.

The Chain Rule

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x))\,g'(x) \]

In Leibniz notation, if \(y\) depends on \(u\) and \(u\) depends on \(x\), the rule is below.

\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx} \]

The Chain Rule is Ever-Present

\[ \begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{dx}\left[u^n\right] = n u^{n - 1} u' \\ &\frac{d}{dx}\left[e^u\right] = e^u u' \\ &\frac{d}{dx}[\ln u] = \frac{u'}{u} \\ &\frac{d}{dx}\left[a^u\right] = (\ln a)\,a^u u' \end{aligned} \]

Differentiate \(y = \left(x^3 + 2x\right)^4\).

\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = 4\left(x^3 + 2x\right)^3\left(3x^2 + 2\right) \]

Differentiate \(y = 2^{3x}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{dy}{dx} &= (\ln 2)\,2^{3x} \cdot 3 \\ &= 3(\ln 2)\,2^{3x} \end{aligned} \]

Find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y = e^{-11x^2}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{dy}{dx} &= e^{-11x^2} \cdot (-22x) \\ &= -22x\,e^{-11x^2} \end{aligned} \]

Differentiate \(f(x) = -\dfrac{7}{\left(4x^3 - 13\right)^4}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} f(x) &= -7\left(4x^3 - 13\right)^{-4} \\ f'(x) &= 28\left(4x^3 - 13\right)^{-5} \cdot 12x^2 \\ &= \frac{336x^2}{\left(4x^3 - 13\right)^5} \end{aligned} \]

Find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y = \ln\left(x^{12} + 21\right)\).

\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{12x^{11}}{x^{12} + 21} \]

Differentiate \(f(\theta) = \sec(6\theta)\).

\[ f'(\theta) = 6\sec(6\theta)\tan(6\theta) \]

Differentiate \(f(\theta) = -2\cot^2(3\theta)\).

Rewrite as \(f(\theta) = -2[\cot(3\theta)]^2\). The chain rule is used twice.

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(\theta) &= -4\cot(3\theta) \cdot \left(-\csc^2(3\theta)\right) \cdot 3 \\ &= 12\cot(3\theta)\csc^2(3\theta) \end{aligned} \]
