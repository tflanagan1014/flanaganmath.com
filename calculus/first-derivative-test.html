Lesson 27: The First Derivative Test

A differentiable function is increasing where \(f\,'(x) > 0\) and decreasing where \(f\,'(x) < 0\).

  • As the graph moves through a relative maximum, it switches from increasing to decreasing.
  • As the graph moves through a relative minimum, it switches from decreasing to increasing.
  • Relative extrema occur at critical numbers.

Steps

  1. Find any numbers excluded from the domain of \(f(x)\).
  2. Find where \(f\,'(x)\) is zero or undefined.
  3. Use these numbers to break up the number line into intervals.
  4. Choose a test number in each interval and find the sign of \(f\,'(x)\).
  5. \(f(x)\) is increasing where \(f\,'(x)\) is positive and decreasing where it is negative.
  6. Find the \(y\)-coordinate of each extremum. Always use \(f(x)\) for \(y\)-coordinates.

A relative minimum is where the graph switches from decreasing to increasing. A relative maximum is where it switches from increasing to decreasing.

Find where \(f(x) = x + \dfrac{36}{x}\) is increasing and decreasing, and find and classify its extrema.

The number \(0\) is excluded from the domain.

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= 1 - \frac{36}{x^2} = 0 \\ x^2 &= 36 \\ x &= \pm 6 \end{aligned} \]
Signs of the derivative
Interval\(f\,'(x)\)\(f(x)\) is
\((-\infty, -6)\)+increasing
\((-6, 0)\)−decreasing
\((0, 6)\)−decreasing
\((6, \infty)\)+increasing
\[ \begin{aligned} f(-6) &= -6 - 6 = -12 \\ f(6) &= 6 + 6 = 12 \end{aligned} \]

There is a relative maximum at \((-6, -12)\) and a relative minimum at \((6, 12)\).

Find where \(f(x) = \cos(3x)\) is increasing and decreasing on \((0, 2\pi)\).

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= -3\sin(3x) = 0 \\ x &= \frac{\pi}{3},\ \frac{2\pi}{3},\ \pi,\ \frac{4\pi}{3},\ \frac{5\pi}{3} \end{aligned} \]

Testing a number in each interval, \(f\,'(x)\) alternates in sign, starting negative.

Decreasing on \(\left(0, \frac{\pi}{3}\right)\), \(\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}, \pi\right)\), and \(\left(\frac{4\pi}{3}, \frac{5\pi}{3}\right)\).

Increasing on \(\left(\frac{\pi}{3}, \frac{2\pi}{3}\right)\), \(\left(\pi, \frac{4\pi}{3}\right)\), and \(\left(\frac{5\pi}{3}, 2\pi\right)\).

A Sample Exam Problem

Find where \(f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 + 168}{x - 1}\) is increasing and decreasing, and find its relative extrema.

The number \(1\) is excluded from the domain.

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= \frac{2x(x - 1) - \left(x^2 + 168\right)}{(x - 1)^2} \\ &= \frac{x^2 - 2x - 168}{(x - 1)^2} \\ &= \frac{(x - 14)(x + 12)}{(x - 1)^2} \end{aligned} \]
Signs of the derivative
Interval\(f\,'(x)\)\(f(x)\) is
\((-\infty, -12)\)+increasing
\((-12, 1)\)−decreasing
\((1, 14)\)−decreasing
\((14, \infty)\)+increasing
\[ \begin{aligned} f(-12) &= \frac{312}{-13} = -24 \\ f(14) &= \frac{364}{13} = 28 \end{aligned} \]

There is a relative maximum at \((-12, -24)\) and a relative minimum at \((14, 28)\).
