Lesson 12: Tangent Lines and Rates of Change

The slope of the tangent line is given by the derivative. Then use the point-slope equation and solve for \(y\).

Point-Slope Equation

\[ y - y_1 = m(x - x_1) \]

Find the slope-intercept equation of the tangent line to \(f(x) = 2x^2 + 1\) where \(x = 1\).

From the limit definition, \(f\,'(x) = 4x\).

\[ \begin{aligned} f(1) &= 2(1)^2 + 1 = 3 \\ m = f'(1) &= 4(1) = 4 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} y - 3 &= 4(x - 1) \\ y &= 4x - 1 \end{aligned} \]

Find the slope-intercept equation of the tangent line to \(f(x) = -2x^3\) where \(x = -2\).

Using the power rule, \(f\,'(x) = -6x^2\).

\[ \begin{aligned} f(-2) &= -2(-2)^3 = 16 \\ m = f'(-2) &= -6(-2)^2 = -24 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} y - 16 &= -24(x + 2) \\ y &= -24x - 32 \end{aligned} \]

The function below gives the luminosity of a Sun-like star, in units of the Sun’s current luminosity \(L_\odot\), as a function of time \(x\) in billions of years. Find the rate at which the luminosity is changing when \(x = 2\).

\[ f(x) = 0.0082931x^2 + 0.0270692x + 0.7 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= 0.0165862x + 0.0270692 \\ f'(2) &= 0.0331724 + 0.0270692 \\ &= 0.0602416 \end{aligned} \]

The luminosity is increasing at about \(0.0602\,L_\odot\) per billion years.
