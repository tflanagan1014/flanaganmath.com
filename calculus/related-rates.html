Lesson 23: Related Rates

When we differentiate with respect to \(x\), any other letter gets the chain rule.

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dx}\left[x^4\right] &= 4x^3 \\ \frac{d}{dx}\left[y^4\right] &= 4y^3\,\frac{dy}{dx} \\ \frac{d}{dx}[x] &= 1 \\ \frac{d}{dx}[y] &= \frac{dy}{dx} \end{aligned} \]

Use implicit differentiation to find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y^3 + x - 8x^3 = 100\).

\[ \begin{aligned} 3y^2\,\frac{dy}{dx} + 1 - 24x^2 &= 0 \\ \frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{24x^2 - 1}{3y^2} \end{aligned} \]

In a related rates problem, every variable is a function of time. We differentiate with respect to time, \(t\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}\left[y^4\right] &= 4y^3\,\frac{dy}{dt} \\ \frac{d}{dt}\left[V^4\right] &= 4V^3\,\frac{dV}{dt} \\ \frac{d}{dt}\left[t^4\right] &= 4t^3 \end{aligned} \]

Suppose \(z = 5x^2 + y^3 + 52\), with \(\dfrac{dx}{dt} = 2\) and \(\dfrac{dy}{dt} = -3\). Find \(\dfrac{dz}{dt}\) when \(x = 6\) and \(y = -5\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{dz}{dt} &= 10x\,\frac{dx}{dt} + 3y^2\,\frac{dy}{dt} \\ &= 10(6)(2) + 3(25)(-3) \\ &= 120 - 225 \\ &= -105 \end{aligned} \]

Suppose \(H = x^4\cos(\pi y)\), with \(\dfrac{dx}{dt} = -4\) and \(\dfrac{dy}{dt} = 2\). Find \(\dfrac{dH}{dt}\) when \(x = -2\) and \(y = 5\). Use radians.

Use the product rule and the chain rule.

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{dH}{dt} &= 4x^3\cos(\pi y)\,\frac{dx}{dt} \\ &\qquad {}- \pi x^4\sin(\pi y)\,\frac{dy}{dt} \end{aligned} \]

At this moment, \(\cos(5\pi) = -1\) and \(\sin(5\pi) = 0\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{dH}{dt} &= 4(-8)(-1)(-4) - 0 \\ &= -128 \end{aligned} \]

A sphere is collapsing! Its radius is decreasing at a constant rate of 3 ft/sec. When the radius is 2064 ft, find the rate of change in the volume. Give the exact answer in terms of \(\pi\).

\[ \begin{aligned} V &= \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \\ \frac{dV}{dt} &= 4\pi r^2\,\frac{dr}{dt} \\ &= 4\pi(2064)^2(-3) \\ &= -51{,}121{,}152\pi \end{aligned} \]

The volume is decreasing at \(51{,}121{,}152\pi\) cubic feet per second.

Sample Exam Problems

Suppose \(f(\theta) = 5\sin\theta\) and \(\dfrac{d\theta}{dt} = 3.2\) radians per second. Find \(\dfrac{df}{dt}\) when \(\theta = 2.16\) radians.

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{df}{dt} &= 5\cos\theta\,\frac{d\theta}{dt} \\ &= 5\cos(2.16)(3.2) \\ &\approx -8.891 \end{aligned} \]

An 89 foot pole leans against a wall and begins sliding down. When the base is 39 feet from the wall, the top is sliding down at 3 feet per second. How fast is the base moving away from the wall?

Let \(x\) be the distance from the wall to the base and \(y\) the height of the top.

\[ \begin{aligned} x^2 + y^2 &= 89^2 \\ y &= \sqrt{89^2 - 39^2} = 80 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} 2x\,\frac{dx}{dt} + 2y\,\frac{dy}{dt} &= 0 \\ 39\,\frac{dx}{dt} + 80(-3) &= 0 \\ \frac{dx}{dt} &= \frac{240}{39} = \frac{80}{13} \end{aligned} \]

The base is moving away at \(\frac{80}{13} \approx 6.15\) feet per second.
