Lesson 21: Higher-Order Derivatives

What if we differentiate the derivative?

\[ \frac{d^2 f}{dx^2} = f''(x) \]
\[ \frac{d^3 f}{dx^3} = f'''(x) \]
\[ \frac{d^4 f}{dx^4} = f^{(4)}(x) \]

Find the first four derivatives of \(f(x) = e^x + x^5 - x + 10\).

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= e^x + 5x^4 - 1 \\ f''(x) &= e^x + 20x^3 \\ f'''(x) &= e^x + 60x^2 \\ f^{(4)}(x) &= e^x + 120x \end{aligned} \]

Find \(f\,''(x)\) for \(f(x) = \tan^{-1} x\).

\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= \frac{1}{1 + x^2} \\ &= \left(1 + x^2\right)^{-1} \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} f''(x) &= -\left(1 + x^2\right)^{-2} \cdot 2x \\ &= -\frac{2x}{\left(1 + x^2\right)^2} \end{aligned} \]

Find \(f\,''(x)\) for the function below.

\[ f(x) = -2x^5 - 10x^2 - 19x - 16 + 20e^x \]
\[ \begin{aligned} f'(x) &= -10x^4 - 20x - 19 + 20e^x \\ f''(x) &= -40x^3 - 20 + 20e^x \end{aligned} \]

Find \(f\,''(\theta)\) for \(f(\theta) = \tan(9\pi\theta)\).

\[ f'(\theta) = 9\pi\sec^2(9\pi\theta) \]

Rewrite as \(9\pi[\sec(9\pi\theta)]^2\) and use the chain rule.

\[ \begin{aligned} f''(\theta) &= 9\pi \cdot 2\sec(9\pi\theta) \\ &\qquad \cdot \sec(9\pi\theta)\tan(9\pi\theta) \cdot 9\pi \\ &= 162\pi^2\sec^2(9\pi\theta)\tan(9\pi\theta) \end{aligned} \]
