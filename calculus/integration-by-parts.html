Lesson 44: Integration by Parts

Integration by Parts

\[ \int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du \]

Derive this formula from the product rule.

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dx}[uv] &= u\,\frac{dv}{dx} + v\,\frac{du}{dx} \\ uv &= \int u\,dv + \int v\,du \\ \int u\,dv &= uv - \int v\,du \end{aligned} \]

Idea: Choose \(u\) to be something that gets simpler when you differentiate it.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle \int x e^x\,dx\).

\[ \begin{aligned} u &= x & dv &= e^x\,dx \\ du &= dx & v &= e^x \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \int x e^x\,dx &= x e^x - \int e^x\,dx \\ &= x e^x - e^x + C \end{aligned} \]

Evaluate \(\displaystyle \int_0^\pi x\sin(3x)\,dx\).

\[ \begin{aligned} u &= x & dv &= \sin(3x)\,dx \\ du &= dx & v &= -\tfrac{1}{3}\cos(3x) \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \int_0^\pi x\sin(3x)\,dx &= \left[-\frac{x\cos(3x)}{3}\right]_0^\pi + \frac{1}{3}\int_0^\pi \cos(3x)\,dx \\ &= \frac{\pi}{3} + \left[\frac{\sin(3x)}{9}\right]_0^\pi \\ &= \frac{\pi}{3} \end{aligned} \]

Evaluate \(\displaystyle \int e^{2x}\sin(3x)\,dx\).

Call the integral \(I\). Use parts twice, with \(u = e^{2x}\) both times.

\[ \begin{aligned} I &= -\frac{1}{3}e^{2x}\cos(3x) + \frac{2}{3}\int e^{2x}\cos(3x)\,dx \\ &= -\frac{1}{3}e^{2x}\cos(3x) + \frac{2}{9}e^{2x}\sin(3x) - \frac{4}{9}I \end{aligned} \]

The original integral came back! Solve for \(I\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{13}{9}I &= \frac{e^{2x}}{9}\left(2\sin(3x) - 3\cos(3x)\right) \\ I &= \frac{e^{2x}}{13}\left(2\sin(3x) - 3\cos(3x)\right) + C \end{aligned} \]
