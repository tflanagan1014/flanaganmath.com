Lesson 61: The Ratio Test

Let \(L = \displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|\).

  • If \(L < 1\), the series converges.
  • If \(L > 1\), the series diverges.
  • If \(L = 1\), the test is inconclusive.

The Ratio Test works especially well with factorials and powers.

Apply the Ratio Test to \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{4^k}{(k + 2)!}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| &= \frac{4^{n+1}}{(n + 3)!} \cdot \frac{(n + 2)!}{4^n} \\ &= \frac{4}{n + 3} \end{aligned} \]
\[ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{4}{n + 3} = 0 < 1 \]

The series converges.

Apply the Ratio Test to \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} k\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^k\).

\[ \left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| = \frac{n + 1}{n} \cdot \frac{3}{2} \]
\[ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n + 1}{n} \cdot \frac{3}{2} = \frac{3}{2} > 1 \]

The series diverges.

Apply the Ratio Test to \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{k}{k + 5}\).

\[ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n + 1}{n + 6} \cdot \frac{n + 5}{n} = 1 \]

The Ratio Test is inconclusive. But the Divergence Test settles it: \(\frac{n}{n + 5} \to 1 \neq 0\), so the series diverges.
