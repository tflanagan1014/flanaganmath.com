Lesson 20: Logarithmic Differentiation

\[ \ln(AB) = \ln A + \ln B \]
\[ \ln\left(\frac{A}{B}\right) = \ln A - \ln B \]
\[ \ln A^x = x\ln A \]

We can’t use the power rule on \(y = x^x\), because the power rule needs a constant exponent. We can’t use the rule for \(a^x\) either, because it needs a constant base.

Idea: Take the natural log of both sides, use the power rule for logs, and then differentiate implicitly.

Find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y = x^x\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \ln y &= \ln x^x \\ \ln y &= x\ln x \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{1}{y}\,\frac{dy}{dx} &= (1)(\ln x) + (x)\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) \\ \frac{1}{y}\,\frac{dy}{dx} &= \ln x + 1 \\ \frac{dy}{dx} &= y(\ln x + 1) \\ \frac{dy}{dx} &= x^x(\ln x + 1) \end{aligned} \]

Find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y = x^{\sin x}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \ln y &= \sin x \ln x \\ \frac{1}{y}\,\frac{dy}{dx} &= \cos x\ln x + \frac{\sin x}{x} \\ \frac{dy}{dx} &= x^{\sin x}\left(\cos x\ln x + \frac{\sin x}{x}\right) \end{aligned} \]

Find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) for the function below.

\[ y = \frac{x^{24}\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}{(2x + 1)^6} \]

The log properties turn the products and quotient into sums.

\[ \begin{aligned} \ln y &= 24\ln x + \frac{1}{2}\ln\left(x^2 + 1\right) - 6\ln(2x + 1) \\ \frac{1}{y}\,\frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{24}{x} + \frac{x}{x^2 + 1} - \frac{12}{2x + 1} \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{x^{24}\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}{(2x + 1)^6} \\ &\qquad \cdot \left(\frac{24}{x} + \frac{x}{x^2 + 1} - \frac{12}{2x + 1}\right) \end{aligned} \]

A Sample Exam Problem

Use logarithmic differentiation to find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y = x^{\tan x}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \ln y &= \tan x\ln x \\ \frac{1}{y}\,\frac{dy}{dx} &= \sec^2 x\ln x + \frac{\tan x}{x} \\ \frac{dy}{dx} &= x^{\tan x}\left(\sec^2 x\ln x + \frac{\tan x}{x}\right) \end{aligned} \]
