What does the limit below tell you about the graph of \(f(x)\)?

\[ \lim_{x \to -2} f(x) = \infty \]

The graph has a vertical asymptote at \(x = -2\).

What does the limit below tell you about the graph of \(f(x)\)?

\[ \lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = 4 \]

The graph has a horizontal asymptote at \(y = 4\).

Idea: Divide the top and bottom by the highest power of \(x\) in the denominator. Then use \(\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{1}{x^n} = 0\) for \(n > 0\).

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{2x + 1}{x + 100}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{2x + 1}{x + 100} &= \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{2 + \frac{1}{x}}{1 + \frac{100}{x}} \\ &= \frac{2 + 0}{1 + 0} \\ &= 2 \end{aligned} \]

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{7x + 5000}{x^2 + 1}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{7x + 5000}{x^2 + 1} &= \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\frac{7}{x} + \frac{5000}{x^2}}{1 + \frac{1}{x^2}} \\ &= \frac{0 + 0}{1 + 0} \\ &= 0 \end{aligned} \]
