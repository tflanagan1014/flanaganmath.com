Lesson 11: Rates of Change and Motion

The derivative measures how position changes instantaneously over time. That is velocity. Acceleration is the derivative of velocity, so it is the second derivative of position.

Motion and Derivatives

\[ \begin{aligned} &v(t) = s\,'(t) = \frac{ds}{dt} \\ &a(t) = v'(t) = s''(t) = \frac{d^2 s}{dt^2} \end{aligned} \]

We often use \(s\) for location, position, distance, or displacement.

The height of an object, in feet, after \(t\) seconds is \(h(t) = 900 - 16t^2\). Evaluate and discuss each of the following.

\[ h(0) \]
\[ h(2) \]
\[ h'(t) \]
\[ h'(2) \]
\[ h''(t) \]
\[ \begin{aligned} h(0) &= 900 \\ h(2) &= 900 - 64 = 836 \\ h'(t) &= -32t \\ h'(2) &= -64 \\ h''(t) &= -32 \end{aligned} \]

The object starts 900 feet high. After 2 seconds it is 836 feet high, falling at 64 feet per second. Its acceleration is a constant \(-32\) feet per second per second, which is gravity.

An object moves along the \(x\)-axis. Its signed distance from the origin is \(s(t) = t\sin t\). Find \(s\,'(t)\) and \(s\,''(t)\).

\[ \begin{aligned} s'(t) &= \sin t + t\cos t \\ s''(t) &= \cos t + \cos t - t\sin t \\ &= 2\cos t - t\sin t \end{aligned} \]

A Sample Exam Problem

An object moves along the \(x\)-axis with position \(x(t) = \dfrac{t - 3}{t + 1}\). Find \(x\,'(1)\) and \(x\,''(1)\).

\[ \begin{aligned} x'(t) &= \frac{(1)(t + 1) - (t - 3)(1)}{(t + 1)^2} \\ &= \frac{4}{(t + 1)^2} \\ &= 4(t + 1)^{-2} \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} x''(t) &= -8(t + 1)^{-3} \\ &= -\frac{8}{(t + 1)^3} \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} x'(1) &= \frac{4}{4} = 1 \\ x''(1) &= -\frac{8}{8} = -1 \end{aligned} \]
