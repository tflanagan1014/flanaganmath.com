Lesson 9: Implicit Differentiation

The equation \(y = x^2 + 1\) gives \(y\) explicitly as a function of \(x\). An equation like \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\) defines \(y\) implicitly.

To find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) implicitly, differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\). Every time you differentiate an expression with \(y\), the chain rule multiplies by \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\).

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[x^2\right] = 2x \]
\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[y^2\right] = 2y\,\frac{dy}{dx} \]

Find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) for \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\).

\[ \begin{aligned} 2x + 2y\,\frac{dy}{dx} &= 0 \\ 2y\,\frac{dy}{dx} &= -2x \\ \frac{dy}{dx} &= -\frac{x}{y} \end{aligned} \]

Back in Lesson 1, we could not find the slope of the tangent line to the circle below at \((5, 1)\). Now we can!

\[ (x - 2)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 13 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} 2(x - 2) + 2(y + 1)\,\frac{dy}{dx} &= 0 \\ \frac{dy}{dx} &= -\frac{x - 2}{y + 1} \end{aligned} \]

At \((5, 1)\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{dy}{dx} &= -\frac{5 - 2}{1 + 1} \\ &= -\frac{3}{2} \end{aligned} \]

A Sample Exam Problem

Use implicit differentiation to find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\).

\[ 3xy^2 - x^4 + 6y = 100 \]

Use the product rule on \(3xy^2\).

\[ \begin{aligned} 3y^2 + 6xy\,\frac{dy}{dx} - 4x^3 + 6\,\frac{dy}{dx} &= 0 \\ (6xy + 6)\,\frac{dy}{dx} &= 4x^3 - 3y^2 \\ \frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{4x^3 - 3y^2}{6xy + 6} \end{aligned} \]
