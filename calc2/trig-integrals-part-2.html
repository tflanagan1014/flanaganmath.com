Lesson 13: Trigonometric Integrals, Part 2

  • Power of sine is odd: save one \(\sin x\), change the rest with \(\sin^2\theta = 1 - \cos^2\theta\), and let \(u = \cos x\).
  • Power of cosine is odd: save one \(\cos x\), change the rest with \(\cos^2\theta = 1 - \sin^2\theta\), and let \(u = \sin x\).
  • Both powers are even: use the half-angle identities.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle \int \sin^8 x\cos^3 x\,dx\).

\[ \int \sin^8 x\cos^3 x\,dx = \int \sin^8 x\left(1 - \sin^2 x\right)\cos x\,dx \]

Let \(u = \sin x\).

\[ \int \left(u^8 - u^{10}\right)du = \frac{\sin^9 x}{9} - \frac{\sin^{11} x}{11} + C \]

Evaluate \(\displaystyle \int \sin^5 x\sqrt{\cos x}\,dx\).

\[ \int \sin^5 x\sqrt{\cos x}\,dx = \int \left(1 - \cos^2 x\right)^2\sqrt{\cos x}\,\sin x\,dx \]

Let \(u = \cos x\), so \(du = -\sin x\,dx\).

\[ \begin{aligned} &-\int \left(u^{\frac{1}{2}} - 2u^{\frac{5}{2}} + u^{\frac{9}{2}}\right)du \\ &\quad = -\frac{2}{3}\cos^{\frac{3}{2}} x + \frac{4}{7}\cos^{\frac{7}{2}} x \\ &\qquad\quad {}- \frac{2}{11}\cos^{\frac{11}{2}} x + C \end{aligned} \]

Evaluate \(\displaystyle \int \sin^2 x\cos^2 x\,dx\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \sin^2 x\cos^2 x &= \frac{1 - \cos(2x)}{2} \cdot \frac{1 + \cos(2x)}{2} \\ &= \frac{1 - \cos^2(2x)}{4} \\ &= \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1 + \cos(4x)}{8} \\ &= \frac{1}{8} - \frac{\cos(4x)}{8} \end{aligned} \]
\[ \int \sin^2 x\cos^2 x\,dx = \frac{x}{8} - \frac{\sin(4x)}{32} + C \]
