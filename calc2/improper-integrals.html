Lesson 20: Improper Integrals

An integral is improper in a few cases.

  • An infinite limit of integration, such as \(\displaystyle\int_3^\infty\). When both limits are infinite, split the integral at a convenient point.
  • A vertical asymptote at an endpoint.
  • A vertical asymptote inside the interval. Don’t integrate straight through it! Split the integral there.

In every case, replace the problem spot with a variable, integrate as usual, and take a limit. A finite limit means the integral converges. Otherwise it diverges.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_3^\infty \frac{1}{x^3}\,dx\), or state that it does not exist.

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_3^\infty \frac{dx}{x^3} &= \lim_{t \to \infty} \left[-\frac{1}{2x^2}\right]_3^t \\ &= \lim_{t \to \infty} \left(-\frac{1}{2t^2} + \frac{1}{18}\right) \\ &= \frac{1}{18} \end{aligned} \]

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_2^6 \frac{1}{(x - 3)^2}\,dx\), or state that it does not exist.

There is a vertical asymptote at \(x = 3\), inside the interval. Split there and check the left piece.

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_2^3 \frac{dx}{(x - 3)^2} &= \lim_{t \to 3^-} \left[-\frac{1}{x - 3}\right]_2^t \\ &= \lim_{t \to 3^-} \left(-\frac{1}{t - 3} - 1\right) \\ &= \infty \end{aligned} \]

That piece diverges, so the integral does not exist.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty \frac{1}{81 + 100x^2}\,dx\), or state that it does not exist.

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_0^\infty \frac{dx}{81 + 100x^2} &= \lim_{t \to \infty} \left[\frac{1}{90}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{10x}{9}\right)\right]_0^t \\ &= \frac{1}{90} \cdot \frac{\pi}{2} \\ &= \frac{\pi}{180} \end{aligned} \]

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_5^\infty x e^{-3x}\,dx\), or state that it does not exist.

Integration by parts gives the antiderivative.

\[ \int x e^{-3x}\,dx = -\frac{x e^{-3x}}{3} - \frac{e^{-3x}}{9} + C \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \int_5^\infty x e^{-3x}\,dx &= \lim_{t \to \infty} \left[-\frac{x e^{-3x}}{3} - \frac{e^{-3x}}{9}\right]_5^t \\ &= 0 + \frac{5e^{-15}}{3} + \frac{e^{-15}}{9} \\ &= \frac{16}{9e^{15}} \end{aligned} \]
