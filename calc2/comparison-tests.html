The Comparison Tests

Compare a series to one whose behavior you already know.

  • If \(0 \le a_n \le b_n\) and \(\sum b_k\) converges, then \(\sum a_k\) converges.
  • If \(0 \le b_n \le a_n\) and \(\sum b_k\) diverges, then \(\sum a_k\) diverges.

Does \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k^2 + 3}\) converge or diverge?

\(\dfrac{1}{k^2 + 3} \le \dfrac{1}{k^2}\), and \(\sum \frac{1}{k^2}\) is a convergent p-series. So the series converges.

Suppose all terms of the two series are positive.

The Limit Comparison Test

\[ \begin{aligned} &\text{If } \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n} \text{ is a positive finite number,} \\ &\text{both series converge or both diverge.} \end{aligned} \]

Use \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k^2}\) to decide whether \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{\sqrt{k^4 + 7}}{2k^4 + 1}\) converges.

\(\sum \frac{1}{k^2}\) is a convergent p-series.

\[ \begin{aligned} \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\frac{\sqrt{n^4 + 7}}{2n^4 + 1}}{\frac{1}{n^2}} &= \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n^2\sqrt{n^4 + 7}}{2n^4 + 1} \\ &= \frac{1}{2} \end{aligned} \]

The limit is positive and finite, so the series converges.

Use the harmonic series to decide whether \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k + 5}\) converges.

\[ \begin{aligned} \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\frac{1}{n + 5}}{\frac{1}{n}} &= \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n}{n + 5} \\ &= 1 \end{aligned} \]

The limit is positive and finite, and the harmonic series diverges, so this series diverges.
