The Integral Test and p-Series

The Integral Test

\[ \begin{aligned} &\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} a_k \text{ and } \int_1^\infty f(x)\,dx \\ &\text{either both converge or both diverge.} \end{aligned} \]

This requires \(f(k) = a_k\), and \(f\) must be continuous, positive, and nonincreasing.

Consider \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{\infty} \frac{k}{\left(k^2 - 1\right)^4}\). Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_2^\infty \frac{x}{\left(x^2 - 1\right)^4}\,dx\). What can you conclude?

Let \(u = x^2 - 1\), so \(du = 2x\,dx\). When \(x = 2\), \(u = 3\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_2^\infty \frac{x\,dx}{\left(x^2 - 1\right)^4} &= \frac{1}{2}\int_3^\infty u^{-4}\,du \\ &= \frac{1}{2}\lim_{t \to \infty} \left[-\frac{1}{3u^3}\right]_3^t \\ &= \frac{1}{162} \end{aligned} \]

The integral converges, so the series converges. (The series does not equal 1/162. The test only tells us it converges.)

p-Series

\[ \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k^p} \]
  • If \(p > 1\), the series converges.
  • If \(p \le 1\), the series diverges.

Consider \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k^6}\). Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_1^\infty \frac{dx}{x^6}\). What can you conclude?

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_1^\infty x^{-6}\,dx &= \lim_{t \to \infty} \left[-\frac{1}{5x^5}\right]_1^t \\ &= \frac{1}{5} \end{aligned} \]

The integral converges, so the series converges. It is a p-series with \(p = 6 > 1\).

Consider the harmonic series \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k}\). Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_1^\infty \frac{dx}{x}\). What can you conclude?

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_1^\infty \frac{dx}{x} &= \lim_{t \to \infty} \ln t \\ &= \infty \end{aligned} \]

The integral diverges, so the harmonic series diverges.
