Lesson 12: Trigonometric Integrals, Part 1

Even Powers

\[ \begin{aligned} &\sin^2\theta = \frac{1 - \cos(2\theta)}{2} \\ &\cos^2\theta = \frac{1 + \cos(2\theta)}{2} \end{aligned} \]

Odd powers: Save one factor of \(\sin x\) or \(\cos x\). Change the remaining even power with \(\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1\), then substitute \(u = \cos x\) or \(u = \sin x\).

Evaluate \(\displaystyle \int \cos^2 x\,dx\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \int \cos^2 x\,dx &= \int \frac{1 + \cos(2x)}{2}\,dx \\ &= \frac{x}{2} + \frac{\sin(2x)}{4} + C \end{aligned} \]

Evaluate \(\displaystyle \int \sin^4 x\,dx\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \sin^4 x &= \left(\frac{1 - \cos(2x)}{2}\right)^2 \\ &= \frac{1}{4}\left(1 - 2\cos(2x) + \cos^2(2x)\right) \\ &= \frac{1}{4}\left(1 - 2\cos(2x) + \frac{1 + \cos(4x)}{2}\right) \\ &= \frac{3}{8} - \frac{\cos(2x)}{2} + \frac{\cos(4x)}{8} \end{aligned} \]
\[ \int \sin^4 x\,dx = \frac{3x}{8} - \frac{\sin(2x)}{4} + \frac{\sin(4x)}{32} + C \]

Evaluate \(\displaystyle \int \cos^5 x\,dx\).

\[ \int \cos^5 x\,dx = \int \left(1 - \sin^2 x\right)^2\cos x\,dx \]

Let \(u = \sin x\), so \(du = \cos x\,dx\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \int \left(1 - u^2\right)^2 du &= \int \left(1 - 2u^2 + u^4\right)du \\ &= \sin x - \frac{2\sin^3 x}{3} + \frac{\sin^5 x}{5} + C \end{aligned} \]
