Geometric Series

Geometric Series

\[ \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} ar^{k - 1} = a + ar + ar^2 + ar^3 + \cdots \]
  • If \(|r| \ge 1\), the series diverges.
  • If \(|r| < 1\), the series converges to \(\dfrac{a}{1 - r}\).

Find \(S_4\) for \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2} \cdot 3^{k - 1}\). Then explain why the series diverges.

\[ \begin{aligned} S_4 &= \frac{1}{2}(1 + 3 + 9 + 27) \\ &= 20 \end{aligned} \]

The ratio is \(r = 3\), and \(|r| \ge 1\), so the series diverges.

Find \(S_5\) for \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 6\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k - 1}\). Then explain why it converges, and find its sum.

\[ \begin{aligned} S_5 &= 6\left(1 + \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{4} + \tfrac{1}{8} + \tfrac{1}{16}\right) \\ &= 11.625 \end{aligned} \]

The ratio is \(r = \frac{1}{2}\), and \(|r| < 1\), so it converges.

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{a}{1 - r} &= \frac{6}{1 - \frac{1}{2}} \\ &= 12 \end{aligned} \]

Does \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{\infty} 5\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{k - 1}\) converge or diverge? If it converges, find its sum.

This series starts at \(k = 2\), so its first term is \(a = 5\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{5}{3}\). The ratio is \(r = \frac{1}{3}\), so it converges.

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{a}{1 - r} &= \frac{\frac{5}{3}}{1 - \frac{1}{3}} \\ &= \frac{5}{2} \end{aligned} \]
