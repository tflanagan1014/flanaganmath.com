Number Bases in Positional Systems

Because of our ten fingers, the choice of ten as the base of our positional system seems to be a natural choice. However, it isn’t base ten that computers use to process information. Computer technology encodes information using a binary, or base two, system of \(\mathbf{0}\)’s and \(\mathbf{1}\)’s. In this section, we study numeration systems that, like binary, use a base other than ten.

This system uses only \(\mathbf{0}\)’s and \(\mathbf{1}\)’s. The place values are determined by the powers of two.

Example #4

Convert the base ten Hindu-Arabic numeral \(\mathbf{89}\bse{ten}\) to base six.

  • The place values will be the powers of six: \(\pv{1}\), \(\pv{6}\), \(\pv{36}\), \(\pv{216}\), \(\pv{1296}\), …
  • Choose the power that is closest to \(\mathbf{89}\), but also smaller than \(\mathbf{89}\)!
  • The choice should be \(\pv{36}\). Next, divide by \(\pv{36}\), using long division!
\(\begin{array}{r} \qt{2} \\ \pv{36}\enclose{longdiv}{\mathbf{89}} \\ \underline{-\,\mathbf{72}} \\ \rmd{17} \end{array}\)
↗
\(\begin{array}{r} \qt{2} \\ \pv{6}\enclose{longdiv}{\rmd{17}} \\ \underline{-\,\mathbf{12}} \\ \rmd{5} \end{array}\)
↗
\(\begin{array}{r} \qt{5} \\ \pv{1}\enclose{longdiv}{\rmd{5}} \\ \underline{-\,\mathbf{5}} \\ \rd{0} \end{array}\)
  • Take the remainder and use long division with the next lower power of the base!
  • The place values of each base come from the quotients!
\[ \mathbf{89}\bse{ten} = \qt{2}(\pv{36}) + \qt{2}(\pv{6}) + \qt{5}(\pv{1}) = \ans{\qt{225}\bse{six}} \]
Final Answer 👍
