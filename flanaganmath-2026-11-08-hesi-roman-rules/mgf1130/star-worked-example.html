Star Worked Example

My star is Betelgeuse. Your star will be different, so some of your answers will be too.

Part A – Research

For you, your star, why you chose it, and its distance will be different. Questions 2–4 are the same for everyone: what a light-year is, what an AU is, and the two conversions.

1. What star did you choose, and why did you choose it? What is the distance to your star in light-years? [Note: An approximation is okay.]

I chose Betelgeuse. It is an amazing, large red star that we might see go supernova soon, and it will be amazing if that happens. It is also big and red, just like my favorite dog, Clifford.

Betelgeuse is approximately 600 light-years away.

2. What is a light-year?

A light-year is the distance light travels in one year. It is a unit of distance, not a unit of time.

3. What is an astronomical unit (AU)?

An astronomical unit is the average distance from Earth to the Sun.

4. In class we will research and discuss the below unit conversions.

1 light-year ≈ 63,000 AU

1 AU ≈ 93,000,000 miles

Part C – Dimensional Analysis: Speed

For you, question 1 is the same. In questions 2 and 3, use your star’s distance in miles from Part B in place of 3.5 × 1015 miles. The speeds (about 38,000 mph for Voyager 1 and 600 mph for a jet) and the hours-to-days-to-years steps are the same.

1. What is the Voyager 1 space probe? Approximately how many miles per hour is Voyager 1 traveling?

Voyager 1 is a NASA space probe launched in 1977. It flew past Jupiter and Saturn, and in 2012 it became the first human-made object to reach interstellar space. It is the most distant human-made object from Earth.

Voyager 1 is traveling approximately 38,000 miles per hour.
