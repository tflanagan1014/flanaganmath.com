Lagrange Multipliers

Let’s classify the regions below as closed or open. This is important because, as you learned in Calculus I, a continuous function on a closed and bounded region must have both an absolute minimum and an absolute maximum.

  • \(\{(x, y) \mid x^2 + y^2 \le 1\}\)
    The unit circle, including its interior: closed
  • \(\{(x, y) \mid x^2 + y^2 < 1\}\)
    The interior of the unit circle only: open
  • \(\{(x, y) \mid x^2 + y^2 = 1\}\)
    The unit circle only: closed
  • \(\{(x, y, z) \mid x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \le 1\}\)
    The sphere of radius one, including its interior: closed

We want to find the absolute extrema of a function \(f(x, y, z)\) subject to the constraint \(g(x, y, z) = k\).

Idea: Solve the following system of equations. Then test the solutions to find the maximum and the minimum.

Lagrange Multipliers

\[ \begin{aligned} \nabla f(x, y, z) &= \lambda\,\nabla g(x, y, z) \\ g(x, y, z) &= k \end{aligned} \]
  • The constant \(\lambda\) is called the Lagrange multiplier.
  • At the points we test, we need \(\nabla g \neq \mathbf{0}\).
  • The constraint is an equation, so it defines a closed region. When that region is also bounded, like a sphere, the absolute maximum and minimum must exist.

An Example

Find the absolute extrema of the function below subject to the constraint \(x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 3\).

\[ f(x, y, z) = x + y + z \]

Here \(g(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2\) and \(k = 3\). Find the gradients.

\[ \nabla f = \langle 1,\ 1,\ 1 \rangle \]
\[ \nabla g = \langle 2x,\ 2y,\ 2z \rangle \]

Set \(\nabla f = \lambda\,\nabla g\), and compare components.

\[ 1 = 2\lambda x \]
\[ 1 = 2\lambda y \]
\[ 1 = 2\lambda z \]

Since \(\lambda \neq 0\), these give \(x = y = z = \frac{1}{2\lambda}\). Substitute into the constraint.

\[ x^2 + x^2 + x^2 = 3 \]
\[ x^2 = 1 \]
\[ x = \pm 1 \]

So the points to test are \((1, 1, 1)\) and \((-1, -1, -1)\). Note \(\nabla g \neq \mathbf{0}\) at both.

\[ f(1, 1, 1) = 3 \]
\[ f(-1, -1, -1) = -3 \]

The absolute maximum is \(3\), at \((1, 1, 1)\), and the absolute minimum is \(-3\), at \((-1, -1, -1)\).
