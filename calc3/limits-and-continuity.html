Lesson 6: Limits and Continuity of Vector-Valued Functions

Consider the below vector-valued function, \(\mathbf{r}(t)\).

\[ \mathbf{r}(t) = x(t)\mathbf{i} + y(t)\mathbf{j} + z(t)\mathbf{k} \]

The limit of this function as \(t\) approaches \(t_0\) is defined below, provided these limits exist.

Limit of a Vector-Valued Function

\[ \begin{aligned} \lim_{t \to t_0} \mathbf{r}(t) &= \left[ \lim_{t \to t_0} x(t) \right] \mathbf{i} \\ &\qquad {}+ \left[ \lim_{t \to t_0} y(t) \right] \mathbf{j} \\ &\qquad {}+ \left[ \lim_{t \to t_0} z(t) \right] \mathbf{k} \end{aligned} \]

This can also be expressed via angle bracket notation, for \(\vec{r}(t) = \langle x(t), y(t), z(t) \rangle\).

\[ \lim_{t \to t_0} \vec{r}(t) = \left\langle \lim_{t \to t_0} x(t),\ \lim_{t \to t_0} y(t),\ \lim_{t \to t_0} z(t) \right\rangle \]

An Example

Consider the below vector-valued function, \(\vec{r}(t)\). Evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{t \to 0} \vec{r}(t)\).

\[ \vec{r}(t) = \left\langle \frac{t^2 - t}{t},\ \frac{\sin t}{t},\ 2t \right\rangle \]

Find the limit of each component.

\[ \begin{aligned} \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{t^2 - t}{t} &= \lim_{t \to 0} (t - 1) \\ &= -1 \\ \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\sin t}{t} &= 1 \\ \lim_{t \to 0} 2t &= 0 \end{aligned} \]

Putting the components together gives the limit of the function.

\[ \lim_{t \to 0} \vec{r}(t) = \langle -1,\ 1,\ 0 \rangle \]

Note: As a follow-up question, I will often ask for the magnitude of a vector you have found.

\[ \begin{aligned} \left\| \lim_{t \to 0} \vec{r}(t) \right\| &= \sqrt{(-1)^2 + 1^2 + 0^2} \\ &= \sqrt{2} \end{aligned} \]

Consider the below vector-valued function, \(\mathbf{r}(t)\).

\[ \mathbf{r}(t) = x(t)\mathbf{i} + y(t)\mathbf{j} + z(t)\mathbf{k} \]

The function is continuous at the real number \(t_0\) if all three of the following are true.

  • \(\mathbf{r}(t_0)\) exists.
  • \(\displaystyle\lim_{t \to t_0} \mathbf{r}(t)\) exists.
  • \(\displaystyle\lim_{t \to t_0} \mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{r}(t_0)\).

An Example

Explain why the below function is continuous at zero.

\[ \mathbf{r}(t) = t^2\mathbf{i} + (1 + t)\mathbf{j} + (\sin t)\mathbf{k} \]

The function is continuous at zero because the three requirements for continuity hold, as shown below.

First, \(\mathbf{r}(0)\) exists.

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{r}(0) &= 0^2\mathbf{i} + (1 + 0)\mathbf{j} + (\sin 0)\mathbf{k} \\ &= 0\mathbf{i} + 1\mathbf{j} + 0\mathbf{k} \\ &= \mathbf{j} \end{aligned} \]

Second, \(\displaystyle\lim_{t \to 0} \mathbf{r}(t)\) exists.

\[ \begin{aligned} \lim_{t \to 0} \mathbf{r}(t) &= \left[ \lim_{t \to 0} t^2 \right] \mathbf{i} \\ &\qquad {}+ \left[ \lim_{t \to 0} (1 + t) \right] \mathbf{j} \\ &\qquad {}+ \left[ \lim_{t \to 0} \sin t \right] \mathbf{k} \\ &= 0\mathbf{i} + 1\mathbf{j} + 0\mathbf{k} \\ &= \mathbf{j} \end{aligned} \]

Third, the limit equals the value of the function, since both are \(\mathbf{j}\).

\[ \lim_{t \to 0} \mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{r}(0) \]
