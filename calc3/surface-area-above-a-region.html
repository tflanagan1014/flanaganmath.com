Lesson 33: Surface Area above a Region

The figure shows a surface \(z = f(x, y)\) above a region \(R\) in the \(xy\)-plane. The area of the surface is given by the double integral below.

Surface Area

\[ \text{Surface Area} = \iint_R \sqrt{\left[f_x\right]^2 + \left[f_y\right]^2 + 1}\,dA \]

An Example

Consider the surface defined by the equation below. Find the area of the part of this surface that lies above the region in the \(xy\)-plane defined by \(0 \le x \le 3\) and \(0 \le y \le 4\). State the exact answer in simplest form, using radical notation if necessary.

\[ z = f(x, y) = 6x + 7y \]
\[ f_x = 6 \]
\[ f_y = 7 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &\text{Surface Area} \\ &\quad = \int_0^4 \int_0^3 \sqrt{6^2 + 7^2 + 1}\,dx\,dy \\ &\quad = \int_0^4 \int_0^3 \sqrt{86}\,dx\,dy \\ &\quad = \sqrt{86} \cdot 3 \cdot 4 \\ &\quad = 12\sqrt{86} \end{aligned} \]

Since the integrand is the constant \(\sqrt{86}\), the double integral is \(\sqrt{86}\) times the area of the region, \(3 \cdot 4 = 12\). The surface area is \(12\sqrt{86} \approx 111.28\).
