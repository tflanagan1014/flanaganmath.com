Integrals over Rectangular Regions

Consider the solid lying above or below a region in the \(xy\)-plane, where the height of the surface is given by a function \(z = f(x, y)\). In the previous lesson we found the exact volume of this solid as the limit of a Riemann sum.

\[ \lim_{(m,n) \to \infty} \sum_{j=1}^{n} \sum_{i=1}^{m} f(i\,\Delta x,\ j\,\Delta y)\,\Delta x\,\Delta y \]

In this lesson we learn to evaluate this with integrals and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus. We also evaluate double and triple integrals over rectangular regions in general, along with some important notation and terminology.

An Example

Evaluate the integral below.

\[ \int_0^4 \int_0^8 x^2 y^3\,dx\,dy \]

Integrate the inside integral first, with respect to \(x\), treating \(y\) as a constant.

\[ \begin{aligned} &\int_0^4 \int_0^8 x^2 y^3\,dx\,dy \\ &\quad = \int_0^4 \left[ \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_0^8 y^3\,dy \\ &\quad = \int_0^4 \frac{512}{3}\,y^3\,dy \\ &\quad = \frac{512}{3} \left[ \frac{y^4}{4} \right]_0^4 \\ &\quad = \frac{512}{3} \cdot 64 \\ &\quad = \frac{32768}{3} \end{aligned} \]

This matches the limit of the Riemann sum from the previous lesson!

An Example

Evaluate the integral below.

\[ \int_0^8 \int_0^4 x^2 y^3\,dy\,dx \]

This time, integrate with respect to \(y\) first, treating \(x\) as a constant.

\[ \begin{aligned} &\int_0^8 \int_0^4 x^2 y^3\,dy\,dx \\ &\quad = \int_0^8 x^2 \left[ \frac{y^4}{4} \right]_0^4 dx \\ &\quad = \int_0^8 64x^2\,dx \\ &\quad = 64 \left[ \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_0^8 \\ &\quad = \frac{32768}{3} \end{aligned} \]

Changing the order of integration gives the same result.

Fubini’s Theorem says that for a continuous function on a rectangular region, we can integrate in either order, as long as the limits of integration match the order, so they still describe the same region. The result is the same.

Fubini's Theorem

\[ \begin{aligned} &\int_c^d \int_a^b f(x, y)\,dx\,dy \\ &\quad = \int_a^b \int_c^d f(x, y)\,dy\,dx \end{aligned} \]

An Example

Let \(R\) be the region defined by the inequalities \(0 \le x \le 4\) and \(-2 \le y \le 2\). Evaluate the double integral below.

\[ \iint_R \left( x^3 + 6y^3 \right) dA \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &\int_{-2}^{2} \int_0^4 \left( x^3 + 6y^3 \right) dx\,dy \\ &\quad = \int_{-2}^{2} \left[ \frac{x^4}{4} + 6y^3 x \right]_0^4 dy \\ &\quad = \int_{-2}^{2} \left( 64 + 24y^3 \right) dy \\ &\quad = \Big[ 64y + 6y^4 \Big]_{-2}^{2} \\ &\quad = (128 + 96) - (-128 + 96) \\ &\quad = 256 \end{aligned} \]

An Example

Let \(E\) be the solid defined by the inequalities \(0 \le x \le 4\), \(0 \le y \le 2\), and \(-1 \le z \le 2\). Evaluate the triple integral below.

\[ \iiint_E (1 + xyz)\,dV \]
\[ \begin{aligned} &\int_{-1}^{2} \int_0^2 \int_0^4 (1 + xyz)\,dx\,dy\,dz \\ &\quad = \int_{-1}^{2} \int_0^2 \left[ x + \frac{x^2 yz}{2} \right]_0^4 dy\,dz \\ &\quad = \int_{-1}^{2} \int_0^2 (4 + 8yz)\,dy\,dz \\ &\quad = \int_{-1}^{2} \Big[ 4y + 4y^2 z \Big]_0^2 dz \\ &\quad = \int_{-1}^{2} (8 + 16z)\,dz \\ &\quad = \Big[ 8z + 8z^2 \Big]_{-1}^{2} \\ &\quad = (16 + 32) - (-8 + 8) \\ &\quad = 48 \end{aligned} \]
