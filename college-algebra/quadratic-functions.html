Module 4 – Topic 7

Quadratic Functions

A quadratic function has the form \(f(x)=ax^2+bx+c\), with \(a\ne0\).

Its graph is a U-shaped curve called a parabola. The parabola is symmetric: the vertical line through the vertex splits it into two matching halves.

If \(a\gt0\), the parabola opens up, and the vertex is its lowest point, a minimum. If \(a\lt0\), it opens down, and the vertex is its highest point, a maximum.

The vertex is the turning point. Its \(x\)-coordinate is \(x=-\dfrac{b}{2a}\). Its \(y\)-coordinate is found by plugging that \(x\) into \(f\). The range starts or ends at the \(y\)-coordinate of the vertex.

\(x\)-Coordinate of the Vertex

\[ x=-\frac{b}{2a} \]

Example 1

For \(f(x)=-3x^2+12x+1\), does it open up or down? Find the vertex, the maximum or minimum value, and the range.

Here \(a=-3\), \(b=12\), and \(c=1\). Since \(a\lt0\), the parabola opens down, so the vertex is a maximum.

\[ x=-\frac{b}{2a}=-\frac{12}{2(-3)}=2 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} f(2) &= -3(2)^2+12(2)+1 \\ &= -12+24+1 \\ &= 13 \end{aligned} \]

Vertex: \((2,\,13)\)

Maximum value: \(13\)

Range: \((-\infty,\,13]\)

Revenue is price times quantity, \(R=xp\). When the quantity sold depends on the price, \(R\) becomes a quadratic function of the price that opens down, so the maximum revenue is at the vertex.

Example 2

The demand equation for a product is \(x=-2p+300\), where \(p\) is the price in dollars. Express the revenue \(R\) as a function of \(p\). Find the price that maximizes revenue, the maximum revenue, and the units sold at that price.

Substitute the demand equation into \(R=xp\).

\[ \begin{aligned} R(p) &= xp \\ &= (-2p+300)p \\ &= -2p^2+300p \end{aligned} \]

Here \(a=-2\) and \(b=300\). The parabola opens down, so the maximum is at the vertex.

\[ p=-\frac{b}{2a}=-\frac{300}{2(-2)}=75 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} R(75) &= -2(75)^2+300(75) \\ &= -11{,}250+22{,}500 \\ &= 11{,}250 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} x &= -2(75)+300 \\ &= 150 \end{aligned} \]

A price of \(\$75\) maximizes revenue. The maximum revenue is \(\$11{,}250\), with \(150\) units sold.
