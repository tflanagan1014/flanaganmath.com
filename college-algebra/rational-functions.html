Module 4 – Topic 3

Working with Functions: Rational Functions

A rational function is a fraction whose top and bottom are polynomials, such as \(f(x)=\dfrac{x-2}{x-1}\).

The trouble spots are where the denominator is \(0\). Those \(x\)-values are not in the domain. After we factor and simplify, each trouble spot is either a hole (a single missing point) or a vertical asymptote (the graph shoots up or down along a vertical line).

A horizontal asymptote tells us what height the graph approaches as \(x\) gets very large in either direction.

Steps

Factor the top and bottom first.

  • Hole: a factor that cancels. Get its \(y\)-value from the simplified function.
  • Vertical asymptote: where the simplified denominator is \(0\).
  • Horizontal asymptote: compare degrees. Top smaller: \(y=0\). Equal: \(y=\) the ratio of the leading coefficients. Top bigger: none.
  • Intercepts: \(x\)-intercepts where the simplified numerator is \(0\) (never at a hole). \(y\)-intercept: find \(f(0)\).

Example 1

Find the domain, hole, asymptotes, and intercepts of \(f(x)=\dfrac{x^2-4}{x^2+x-2}\).

Factor the top and bottom.

\[ f(x)=\frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{(x+2)(x-1)} \]

Domain: the bottom is \(0\) at \(x=-2\) and \(x=1\), so the domain is \((-\infty,\,-2)\cup(-2,\,1)\cup(1,\,\infty)\).

The factor \(x+2\) cancels, so the simplified function is

\[ f(x)=\frac{x-2}{x-1}, \quad x\ne-2 \]

Hole: at \(x=-2\). Use the simplified function for the \(y\)-value.

\[ \frac{-2-2}{-2-1}=\frac{-4}{-3}=\frac43 \]

The hole is \(\left(-2,\,\tfrac43\right)\).

Vertical asymptote: the simplified denominator is \(0\) at \(x=1\), so \(x=1\).

Horizontal asymptote: the degrees are equal (both \(2\)), and the leading coefficients are \(1\) and \(1\), so \(y=\tfrac11=1\).

\(x\)-intercept: the simplified numerator is \(0\) at \(x=2\), so \((2,\,0)\).

\(y\)-intercept: \(f(0)=\dfrac{0-2}{0-1}=2\), so \((0,\,2)\).

Example 2

Find the asymptotes and intercepts of \(f(x)=\dfrac{2x^2+1}{x-3}\).

Nothing factors, so nothing cancels, and there are no holes.

Vertical asymptote: the bottom is \(0\) at \(x=3\), so \(x=3\).

Horizontal asymptote: the top has degree \(2\) and the bottom has degree \(1\). The top is bigger, so there is none.

\(x\)-intercepts: set the numerator equal to \(0\).

\[ \begin{aligned} 2x^2+1 &= 0 \\ x^2 &= -\tfrac12 \end{aligned} \]

There is no real solution, so there are no \(x\)-intercepts.

\(y\)-intercept: \(f(0)=\dfrac{0+1}{0-3}=-\dfrac13\), so \(\left(0,\,-\tfrac13\right)\).
