Module 4 – Topic 1

Reading Graphs and Piecewise-Defined Functions

  • The domain is the set of all \(x\)-values on the graph. Read it from left to right.
  • The range is the set of all \(y\)-values on the graph. Read it from bottom to top.
  • An \(x\)-intercept is a point where the graph crosses or touches the \(x\)-axis. It has the form \((a,\,0)\).
  • A \(y\)-intercept is a point where the graph crosses the \(y\)-axis. It has the form \((0,\,b)\).
  • \(f(a)\) is the \(y\)-value of the point on the graph where \(x = a\).
  • A filled circle \(\bullet\) means the point is included on the graph.
  • An open circle \(\circ\) means the point is not included on the graph.
  • An arrow at the end of a graph means the graph continues forever in that direction.
  • An asymptote is a line the graph gets closer and closer to. We draw it as a dashed line and name it by its equation.

The graph of \(f\) is shown below. Each tick mark represents \(1\) unit. The asymptote is shown as a dotted line.

  1. Equation of the asymptote
  2. Domain
  3. Range
  4. \(x\)-intercept(s)
  5. \(y\)-intercept
  6. \(f(-2)\), \(f(1)\), and \(f(5)\)
  7. For what value(s) of \(x\) is \(f(x) = -1\)?
  8. What is the maximum value of \(f\)?
  9. Where does the maximum occur?
  1. Asymptote: \(y = -2\)
  2. Domain: \((-\infty,\,5]\). The arrow on the left means the graph goes on forever to the left, and the filled endpoint at \(x = 5\) is included.
  3. Range: \((-2,\,3]\). The graph never reaches the asymptote \(y = -2\), and its highest point has \(y = 3\).
  4. \(x\)-intercepts: \((-1,\,0)\) and \((4,\,0)\)
  5. \(y\)-intercept: \((0,\,2)\)
  6. \(f(-2) = -1\), \(f(1) = 3\), \(f(5) = -1\)
  7. \(x = -2\) and \(x = 5\), the two points on the graph at height \(-1\)
  8. The maximum value is \(3\).
  9. It occurs at \(x = 1\).

An open circle means that point is not on the graph. When an open circle and a filled circle have the same \(x\)-value, the filled circle gives the function value there.

Example 2

The graph of \(f\) is shown below. Each tick mark represents \(1\) unit.

  1. Domain
  2. Range
  3. \(x\)-intercept(s)
  4. \(y\)-intercept
  5. \(f(-2)\), \(f(1)\), and \(f(3)\)
  6. Where is the open circle?
  1. Domain: \((-\infty,\,\infty)\). Every \(x\)-value has a point: the left piece covers \(x \lt 1\), and the filled circle starts the right piece at \(x = 1\).
  2. Range: \([-4,\,\infty)\). The lowest point is \((0,\,-4)\), and the arrows go up forever.
  3. \(x\)-intercept: \((-2,\,0)\)
  4. \(y\)-intercept: \((0,\,-4)\)
  5. \(f(-2) = 0\), \(f(1) = 2\) (the filled circle), \(f(3) = 4\)
  6. The open circle is at \((1,\,-3)\).

A piecewise-defined function uses different formulas on different parts of its domain.

To graph one: graph each piece only on its own interval. At each endpoint, use a filled circle for \(\le\) or \(\ge\), and an open circle for \(\lt\) or \(\gt\).

Example 3

Graph the function, then answer the questions.

\[ f(x) = \begin{cases} 2x + 4 & \text{if } x \le -1 \\ -x + 1 & \text{if } x > -1 \end{cases} \]
  1. \(f(-3)\), \(f(-1)\), and \(f(2)\)
  2. Domain
  3. Range
  4. \(x\)-intercept(s)
  5. \(y\)-intercept
Piece 1: \(x \le -1\)
\(x\)\(f(x)\)
\(-3\)\(-2\)
\(-2\)\(0\)
\(-1\)\(2\)
Piece 2: \(x > -1\)
\(x\)\(f(x)\)
\(-1\)\(2\ \text{(open)}\)
\(0\)\(1\)
\(1\)\(0\)
\(2\)\(-1\)

At \(x = -1\), piece 1 has a filled circle at \((-1,\,2)\), and piece 2 has an open circle at the same point. The filled circle fills it in, so the graph has no break.

  1. \(f(-3) = -2\), \(f(-1) = 2\), \(f(2) = -1\)
  2. Domain: \((-\infty,\,\infty)\)
  3. Range: \((-\infty,\,2]\)
  4. \(x\)-intercepts: \((-2,\,0)\) and \((1,\,0)\)
  5. \(y\)-intercept: \((0,\,1)\)

This piecewise function is not continuous. Its graph has jumps, so you cannot draw it without lifting your pencil.

Example 4

Graph the function, then answer the questions.

\[ f(x) = \begin{cases} x + 4 & \text{if } x < -2 \\ 1 & \text{if } -2 \le x \le 1 \\ 5 - x & \text{if } x > 1 \end{cases} \]
  1. \(f(-2)\), \(f(1)\), and \(f(3)\)
  2. Domain
  3. Range
  4. \(x\)-intercept(s)
  5. \(y\)-intercept
Piece 1: \(x \lt -2\)
\(x\)\(f(x)\)
\(-4\)\(0\)
\(-3\)\(1\)
\(-2\)\(2\ \text{(open)}\)
Piece 2: \(-2 \le x \le 1\)
\(x\)\(f(x)\)
\(-2\)\(1\)
\(0\)\(1\)
\(1\)\(1\)
Piece 3: \(x \gt 1\)
\(x\)\(f(x)\)
\(1\)\(4\ \text{(open)}\)
\(3\)\(2\)
\(5\)\(0\)
  1. \(f(-2) = 1\), \(f(1) = 1\), \(f(3) = 2\). At \(x = -2\) and \(x = 1\), use the filled circles.
  2. Domain: \((-\infty,\,\infty)\)
  3. Range: \((-\infty,\,4)\). The open circle at \((1,\,4)\) is not included, and everything below it is reached.
  4. \(x\)-intercepts: \((-4,\,0)\) and \((5,\,0)\)
  5. \(y\)-intercept: \((0,\,1)\)
