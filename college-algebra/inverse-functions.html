Inverse Functions

An inverse function reverses what a function does. For example, \(f(x)=x+3\) adds \(3\), so its inverse subtracts \(3\): \(f^{-1}(x)=x-3\).

If \(f\) sends \(a\) to \(b\), then \(f^{-1}\) sends \(b\) back to \(a\). So if \((a,\,b)\) is on the graph of \(f\), then \((b,\,a)\) is on the graph of \(f^{-1}\). Inputs and outputs trade places.

Because inputs and outputs trade places, the domain of \(f\) is the range of \(f^{-1}\), and the range of \(f\) is the domain of \(f^{-1}\).

Only a one-to-one function has an inverse: each output must come from exactly one input.

To find \(f^{-1}\): replace \(f(x)\) with \(y\), switch \(x\) and \(y\) (this trades inputs and outputs), and solve for \(y\).

Example 1

Find the inverse of \(f(x)=3x+5\).

Replace \(f(x)\) with \(y\), switch \(x\) and \(y\), and solve for \(y\).

\[ \begin{aligned} y &= 3x+5 \\ x &= 3y+5 \\ x-5 &= 3y \\ \frac{x-5}{3} &= y \end{aligned} \]
\[ f^{-1}(x)=\frac{x-5}{3} \]

Example 2

Let \(f(x)=\dfrac{3x+1}{x-2}\). Find the domain of \(f\), the inverse, and the range of \(f\).

Domain of \(f\): the bottom is \(0\) at \(x=2\), so the domain is \((-\infty,\,2)\cup(2,\,\infty)\).

Inverse: replace \(f(x)\) with \(y\) and switch \(x\) and \(y\).

\[ x=\frac{3y+1}{y-2} \]

Clear the fraction, gather the \(y\) terms on one side, and factor out \(y\).

\[ \begin{aligned} x(y-2) &= 3y+1 \\ xy-2x &= 3y+1 \\ xy-3y &= 2x+1 \\ y(x-3) &= 2x+1 \\ y &= \frac{2x+1}{x-3} \end{aligned} \]
\[ f^{-1}(x)=\frac{2x+1}{x-3} \]

Range of \(f\): the range of \(f\) is the domain of \(f^{-1}\). The bottom of \(f^{-1}\) is \(0\) at \(x=3\), so the range of \(f\) is \((-\infty,\,3)\cup(3,\,\infty)\).
