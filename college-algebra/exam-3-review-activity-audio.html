Part 3: Domain of a Function (Topic 3)

Fill in the domain rules

The domain of a function is the set of all blank we are allowed to put into the function.

Rule 1: We can’t divide by blank. What to do: The denominator can’t be blank. Set the denominator equal to 0 and solve. Blank those input values.

Rule 2: We can’t take an even root of a blank number. What to do: The expression inside the even root must be blank. Solve the inequality.

Rule 3: We can’t take the log of blank or a blank number. What to do: The expression inside the log, or natural log, must be blank. Solve the inequality.

Very Important: If an even root is in the denominator, both rules apply, so the expression inside must be blank.

Very Important: Always state the domain in blank.

The domain of a function is the set of all x values, also called inputs, we are allowed to put into the function.

Rule 1: We can’t divide by zero. What to do: The denominator can’t be 0. Set the denominator equal to 0 and solve. Exclude those input values.

Rule 2: We can’t take an even root of a negative number. What to do: The expression inside the even root must be greater than or equal to 0. Solve the inequality.

Rule 3: We can’t take the log of zero or a negative number. What to do: The expression inside the log, or natural log, must be greater than 0. Solve the inequality.

Very Important: If an even root is in the denominator, both rules apply, so the expression inside must be greater than 0.

Very Important: Always state the domain in interval notation.

Find the domain. State your answer in interval notation.
